Product redesign

Spotify Social Hub

Spotify is excellent at personal mixes and weak at sharing them. This study asks what happens if recommending a song stays inside the app — as a post, a comment, or a drop onto a friend’s playlist.

Role
Solo UX redesign
Platform
Mobile
Methods
Journeys, comparative analysis, testing
Output
Lo-fi → interactive proto

The gap

On mobile, sharing a track usually means leaving Spotify. The moment moves to Instagram, Messages, or a group chat, stripped of playback, context, and follow-up. Desktop has a modest friend-activity rail. Mobile does not have an equivalent that feels like a place.

I mapped the current share journey and marked every time the user is asked to become a courier for the product. Those handoffs were the brief.

How I worked

  • Journey mapping of “I heard something and I want a specific person to hear it too.”
  • Comparative looks at how other social apps handle posts, reactions, and lightweight collaboration.
  • Low-fidelity flows for a Social Hub, then higher-fidelity screens for feed, post creation, comments, and notifications.
  • Usability passes focused on discoverability: can someone find sharing without hunting Settings?

The concept

Social Hub is a fifth tab with a job: show what people you follow are listening to, let you post a song with a caption, react in-thread, and add a track to a playlist without a side quest. Presence (“4 followers online”) is there to make sharing feel timely, not performative.

What I would push on in a review

  1. 01
    Don’t compete with listening.

    The hub has to stay secondary to playback. If social becomes the home screen, the product identity slips. I’d keep Now Playing persistent and posting a sheet, not a destination you get lost in.

  2. 02
    Playlist as a social object.

    The gym-playlist prompt in the feed is the most product-y idea in the set: a post that does something in the library, not just a caption under art.

  3. 03
    Privacy is the hidden requirement.

    Friend activity is sensitive. A next iteration would need listening visibility controls before this could be real — not as a footnote, as a first-class setting.
