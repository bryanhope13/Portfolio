About

Curious, practical, and committed to clarity.

I came to UX through Informatics — people, systems, and products that have to work in the real world. That mix still shapes how I work.

I earned a B.S. in Informatics at IU Bloomington with minors in web development and human-centered computing. Along the way I learned I like the part of a project where you sit with someone’s frustration long enough to name it, then design something quieter than the problem.

I’m now pursuing an M.S. in Human-Computer Interaction at IU Indianapolis (expected May 2027). I interned in mobile UX at Vaccine Genie, designing flows and prototypes for iOS and Android, and spent a summer at Zotec Partners turning operational workflow data into reporting leadership could actually use.

I still work a service job. It keeps me honest: people are tired, the line is long, and the “interface” is whatever reduces friction in the next ten seconds. I bring that same bias into screens.

Process

How I like to work

  1. 01 Listen

    Interviews, observation, and walking the current flow until the awkward parts are obvious.

  2. 02 Frame

    Who it’s for, what success looks like, and which constraints are real versus assumed.

  3. 03 Make

    Flows, wireframes, then higher fidelity only when the structure is holding.

  4. 04 Test

    Watch someone use it. Change the confusing parts. Repeat until it feels inevitable.

I care about

  • Accessibility as a default

    Contrast, hierarchy, language, and cognitive load are design decisions. I don’t treat them as a final polish pass.

  • Honest scope

    I’d rather ship a clear flow than a decorated one. Especially early in my career, I want the thinking to be visible.

  • Working with builders

    I prototype in Figma, and I’m comfortable in HTML/CSS and Git. I like sitting next to engineering, not throwing files over a wall.

  • Calm products

    Healthcare, privacy, and public services already carry stress. The UI should not add more.
